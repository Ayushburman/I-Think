CREATINE
C₄H₉N₃O₂ · MW: 131.13 g/mol · CAS: 57-00-1
CREATINE
A naturally occurring nitrogenous organic acid synthesised in the liver, kidney, and pancreas from arginine, glycine, and methionine. Stored predominantly (~95%) in skeletal muscle as phosphocreatine (PCr). The most evidence-backed performance supplement in existence with 500+ peer-reviewed studies.

C₄H₇N₃O · MW: 113.12 g/mol · Creatine breakdown product
CREATININE
A metabolic waste product formed from the spontaneous, non-enzymatic cyclisation and irreversible dehydration of creatine and phosphocreatine in muscle. Filtered freely by the kidney — used clinically as a marker of renal function (GFR).

KEY DISTINCTION: Creatine is the supplement you take. Creatinine is the waste product your body makes when creatine breaks down. High creatinine on a blood test in an athlete taking creatine is almost always benign — always compare against cystatin C or use CKD-EPI formula to assess true GFR. Inform your doctor if you supplement creatine before any kidney function test.
